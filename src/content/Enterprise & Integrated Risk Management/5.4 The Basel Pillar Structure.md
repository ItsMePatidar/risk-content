# 5.4 The Basel Pillar Structure

## Definition

The Basel framework is organised into three pillars. **Pillar 1** sets minimum capital requirements through prescribed calculations for credit, market, and operational risk, alongside leverage and liquidity standards. **Pillar 2** is the supervisory review process, comprising the institution's own ICAAP and the supervisor's assessment of it, and covers material risks that Pillar 1 does not capture. **Pillar 3** requires public disclosure in standardised formats so that market participants can compare institutions and impose discipline through funding costs and valuations.

## Why it matters

The three pillars are frequently taught as a list and are better understood as a sequence with feedback. Pillar 1 produces a comparable number that is deliberately insensitive to institution-specific circumstances. Pillar 2 adds back exactly that sensitivity, and can result in a supervisory capital add-on. Pillar 3 publishes the result, so the market prices the institution accordingly. For a retail lender the practical significance is that its two largest genuine vulnerabilities - banking book interest rate risk and funding liquidity - are barely touched by Pillar 1. IRRBB has no Pillar 1 charge at all. Concentration risk has none. A lender that manages to Pillar 1 alone is managing to the wrong measure of its own risk profile.

## Formula

Mapping the running NBFC's risks across the pillars:

| Risk | Pillar | Treatment | Capital or measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit risk - retail | 1 | Standardized Approach, 125% and 50% risk weights | ₹3,605 cr RWA |
| Operational risk | 1 | Revised Standardised Approach with ILM | ₹485 cr RWA |
| Market risk - trading book | 1 | Standardized duration method | ₹130 cr RWA |
| Leverage | 1 | Leverage ratio, non-risk-based backstop | Exposure-based |
| Liquidity - 30 day | 1 | LCR, minimum 100% | 71.7%, breach |
| Liquidity - structural | 1 | NSFR (banks), ALM buckets (NBFCs) | Ratio and bucket limits |
| IRRBB | 2 | Delta EVE and Delta NII, outlier test at 15% of Tier 1 | ₹58 cr ICAAP add-on |
| Credit concentration | 2 | Internal assessment | ₹42 cr ICAAP add-on |
| Model risk | 2 | Validation framework, ICAAP assessment | ₹31 cr ICAAP add-on |
| Strategic and business risk | 2 | Earnings volatility assessment | ₹26 cr ICAAP add-on |
| Reputational risk | 2 | Qualitative assessment | No capital, mitigation plan |

Pillar 3 disclosure requirements relevant to a retail lender include capital structure and adequacy, risk-weighted assets by risk type and approach, credit quality of assets with asset-class breakdown, non-performing exposures and provisioning, sectoral and geographic concentration, restructured accounts, liquidity ratios, remuneration policy, and the governance and risk management framework itself.

The feedback among the pillars:

```
Pillar 1 requirement  ->  ICAAP assessment (Pillar 2)  ->  Supervisory review
                                                       ->  Possible capital add-on
                                                       ->  Revised Pillar 1 ratios
                                                       ->  Disclosed under Pillar 3
                                                       ->  Market pricing of funding and equity
```

## Worked Example

Example: the full pillar position for the NBFC.

Pillar 1:

```
Total RWA                     = ₹4,220 cr
Regulatory minimum CRAR       = 15.00%
Minimum capital required      = ₹633 cr
Capital held                  = ₹705 cr
CRAR                          = 16.71%  - compliant
Tier 1 ratio                  = 14.69%  - compliant against a 10% minimum
LCR                           = 71.7%   - breach against a 100% minimum
```

Pillar 1 shows a well-capitalised institution failing a liquidity standard.

Pillar 2:

```
Pillar 1 requirement          = ₹633 cr
ICAAP add-ons                 = ₹175 cr
Internal capital requirement  = ₹808 cr
Capital held                  = ₹705 cr
Internal shortfall            = ₹103 cr
```

Pillar 2 shows the same institution as under-capitalised against its own assessment, driven principally by interest rate risk in the banking book and geographic concentration - neither of which appears anywhere in the Pillar 1 number.

Pillar 3: the disclosure would publish a 16.71% CRAR alongside a 71.7% LCR and a GNPA of 2.91%. A funding counterparty reading that combination sees an institution with adequate capital, sound asset quality, and a liquidity position outside the regulatory minimum. The rational response is to shorten the tenor offered or widen the spread - which is precisely the 65 bps spread widening modelled in 2.1, arriving through the market discipline channel the pillar was designed to create.

That sequence is the whole point of the three-pillar architecture. A Pillar 1 liquidity breach becomes a Pillar 3 disclosure, becomes a funding cost, becomes a margin compression, and becomes a capital problem. The pillars are not three separate exercises.

## How it connects

Pillar 1 credit capital is 1.10, market capital is 2.4, operational capital is 4.4, and the liquidity standards are 3.3 and 3.5. Pillar 2 is the ICAAP of 5.1, with add-ons for the IRRBB of 2.2, the concentration of 1.8, and the model risk of 4.7. The governance that produces and challenges all of it is 5.3, and the appetite limits against which the ratios are judged are 5.2. Pillar 3 disclosure connects the framework back to the funding cost analysis in 2.1 and the liquidity stress of 3.4.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Reading the pillars as independent. They form a loop, and the Pillar 3 disclosure of a Pillar 1 breach changes the institution's funding cost, which changes its capital position.
- Assuming Pillar 1 covers the material risks. For a retail lender, IRRBB, concentration, and model risk carry no Pillar 1 charge, and in the example above they account for ₹131 crore of assessed capital need.
- Treating Pillar 2 as advisory. Supervisors can and do impose capital add-ons following review, which become binding requirements.
- Viewing Pillar 3 as a reporting obligation. Its purpose is to let counterparties price the institution, and it works - funding markets react to disclosed ratios faster than supervisors act on them.
- Assuming full Basel applicability. Indian NBFCs operate under Scale Based Regulation, where the specific requirements differ by layer, and the applicable framework must be confirmed against the layer the entity sits in.
