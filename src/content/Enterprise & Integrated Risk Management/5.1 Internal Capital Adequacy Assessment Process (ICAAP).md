# 5.1 Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP)

## Definition

**ICAAP** is the internal process through which a lender identifies every material risk it runs, assesses how much capital it needs to hold against those risks, tests that assessment against stress scenarios, and relates the result to its business plan and risk appetite. It is broader than the regulatory minimum by design: Pillar 1 covers credit, market, and operational risk through prescribed formulas, while ICAAP must also address the risks those formulas do not capture. The output is a board-approved document stating the institution's own view of adequate capital, which the supervisor then reviews and may challenge.

## Why it matters

The Pillar 1 capital calculation is a standardised measure that treats all lenders with the same exposures identically. It does not know that a particular lender's book is concentrated in two states, that its scorecard has drifted, or that its funding is structurally short. ICAAP is where those institution-specific facts get priced in capital terms. It is also the mechanism that connects capital to strategy - a growth plan that cannot be funded within the capital the lender holds or can raise is not a plan, and ICAAP is where that becomes arithmetic rather than assertion. Under Scale Based Regulation, RBI requires NBFCs in the Upper Layer to maintain an ICAAP, and the equivalent obligation has applied to banks under Pillar 2 for years.

## Formula

The process runs in five steps.

Risk identification - a comprehensive inventory of material risks, documented with a rationale for anything excluded. The test is materiality, not whether the risk fits a Pillar 1 category.

Capital assessment - for each material risk, an estimate of capital required. Pillar 1 risks use the regulatory calculation as a floor, with an add-on where the internal view is higher. Pillar 2 risks are assessed with internal methodologies.

```
Internal capital requirement = Pillar 1 requirement + SUM of Pillar 2 add-ons + Capital planning buffer
```

Stress testing - projecting capital and RWA over a three-year horizon under base and stressed scenarios, incorporating the credit, market, and liquidity stresses developed in 1.14, 2.5, and 3.4.

Capital planning - reconciling projected requirement against projected available capital, including retained earnings and any planned issuance.

Governance and use - board approval, integration into decision-making, and independent review. An ICAAP that is written for the supervisor and never referenced in a business decision fails its purpose regardless of its analytical quality.

Risks typically assessed under Pillar 2 in a retail lender: credit concentration (geographic, channel, product), IRRBB, model risk, strategic and business risk, reputational risk, and residual operational risk beyond the standardised charge.

## Worked Example

Example: ICAAP capital assessment for the NBFC carried through Modules 1 to 4. Available capital is ₹705 crore - ₹620 crore Tier 1 and ₹85 crore Tier 2. The regulatory minimum CRAR for NBFCs is 15%.

Pillar 1 position:

| Risk | RWA | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Credit - personal loans at 125% | ₹2,300 cr | 1.10 |
| Credit - home loans at 50% | ₹1,305 cr | 1.10 |
| Operational - revised SA, ILM 1.84 | ₹485 cr | 4.4 |
| Market - trading book | ₹130 cr | 2.4 |
| Total RWA | ₹4,220 cr | |

```
Pillar 1 capital requirement = ₹4,220 cr x 15% = ₹633 cr
Current CRAR = ₹705 cr / ₹4,220 cr = 16.71%
Current Tier 1 ratio = ₹620 cr / ₹4,220 cr = 14.69%
```

Pillar 2 add-ons:

| Risk | Assessment basis | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic concentration - 38% of the book in two states | Internal concentration model | ₹42 cr |
| IRRBB - Delta EVE of ₹74.8 cr at a 200 bps shock | Economic value at risk, per 2.2 | ₹58 cr |
| Model risk - PSI of 0.20 and a 195 bps calibration gap | Provision for under-estimated PD, per 4.7 | ₹31 cr |
| Strategic and business risk | Earnings volatility approach | ₹26 cr |
| Residual operational risk | Scenario analysis beyond the SA charge | ₹18 cr |
| Total Pillar 2 | | ₹175 cr |

```
Internal capital requirement = ₹633 cr + ₹175 cr = ₹808 cr
Available capital             = ₹705 cr
Shortfall                     = ₹103 cr
```

The lender meets its regulatory minimum comfortably and fails its own internal assessment by ₹103 crore. That is the normal and intended outcome of a well-run ICAAP - the internal number should be higher than the regulatory one, because it covers more risks.

Capital projection under stress. The base plan grows the book 25% annually. The stress scenario combines the macro shock from 1.14 (unemployment +2.1 pp, rates +250 bps) with the funding spread widening from 2.1.

| | Base Y1 | Base Y3 | Stress Y1 | Stress Y3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RWA | ₹5,275 cr | ₹8,242 cr | ₹5,275 cr | ₹6,530 cr |
| Post-tax profit | ₹83 cr | ₹129 cr | ₹41 cr | ₹56 cr |
| Available capital | ₹788 cr | ₹1,022 cr | ₹746 cr | ₹869 cr |
| CRAR | 14.94% | 12.40% | 14.14% | 13.31% |

The base case breaches the 15% regulatory minimum in year one and falls to 12.40% by year three. This is not a stress finding - it is a plan finding. A 25% growth rate cannot be funded from retained earnings of ₹83 crore against RWA growth of ₹1,055 crore. The ICAAP conclusion is that the business plan requires approximately ₹340 crore of fresh capital over three years, or the growth rate must fall to roughly 13%, which is what internally generated capital supports.

That conclusion - a growth plan is unfunded - is the most common and most useful output of a first serious ICAAP, and it is invisible from the current 16.71% CRAR.

## How it connects

ICAAP assembles the Pillar 1 numbers from 1.10, 2.4, and 4.4, and the stress scenarios from 1.14, 2.5, and 3.4. The Pillar 2 add-ons cover exactly the risks those earlier modules flagged as unaddressed by Pillar 1 - IRRBB from 2.2, model drift from 4.7, and concentration from 1.8. The capital requirement it produces feeds the capital charge in the pricing build-up of 1.5, closing the loop back to loan pricing. The appetite thresholds against which the projection is judged are set in 5.2, and the pillar architecture is described in 5.4.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Producing an internal number below the regulatory minimum. ICAAP covers more risks than Pillar 1, so an internal requirement lower than the regulatory one indicates the assessment is incomplete rather than that the lender is efficient.
- Treating it as an annual document. Its use is in capital allocation, product pricing, and growth decisions throughout the year, and a supervisor's first question is usually where the ICAAP influenced a decision.
- Stress testing the balance sheet but not the plan. The example above shows the base plan failing before the stress scenario is applied. Growth consumes capital, and the projection must be run on the plan, not on a static book.
- Double counting between Pillar 1 and Pillar 2. Expected loss is covered by provisions, unexpected loss by Pillar 1 capital, and Pillar 2 add-ons should address only what neither captures.
- Assessing risks in isolation and adding them. Simple summation ignores diversification, and most supervisors will accept diversification benefit only where it is evidenced rather than assumed.
