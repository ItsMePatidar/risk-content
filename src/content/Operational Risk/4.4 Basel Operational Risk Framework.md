# 4.4 Basel Operational Risk Framework

## Definition

Basel's approach to operational risk capital has moved from a menu of methods to a single one. The original framework offered the Basic Indicator Approach, the Standardised Approach, and the Advanced Measurement Approach, the last allowing banks to model their own loss distributions. The Basel III finalisation replaced all three with one **Standardised Approach**, sometimes called the SMA, built from a financial-statement proxy for business volume adjusted by the bank's own historical loss experience. AMA was withdrawn because internally modelled operational risk capital proved neither comparable across banks nor stable.

This framework applies to banks. NBFCs in India compute capital adequacy on a credit-risk basis under Scale Based Regulation and are not subject to a Pillar 1 operational risk capital charge, so the worked example below is framed for a retail-focused bank.

## Why it matters

The formula rewards two things a retail lender can control: a low-loss operating history and a business mix that does not inflate the volume proxy. It also contains a feature that matters specifically to high-margin retail lenders - the interest component of the volume proxy is capped at 2.25% of interest-earning assets. A lender earning a 5% net interest margin on unsecured retail has its contribution capped at less than half its actual net interest income, which reduces its operational risk capital relative to a thin-margin lender of the same balance sheet size. Understanding this prevents the common error of assuming operational risk capital scales with profitability.

## Formula

```
BI = ILDC + SC + FC

ILDC = min( |Interest income - Interest expense| , 2.25% x Interest earning assets )
       + Dividend income

SC = max( Other operating income, Other operating expense )
     + max( Fee income, Fee expense )

FC = |Net P&L on the trading book| + |Net P&L on the banking book|
```

where:
- `BI` = business indicator, a financial-statement proxy for operational risk exposure, in ₹
- `ILDC` = interest, lease and dividend component
- `SC` = services component
- `FC` = financial component
- All inputs are three-year averages

The business indicator component applies marginal coefficients by bucket:

```
BIC = SUM over buckets of ( BI in bucket x Coefficient )

Bucket 1: BI up to EUR 1 bn          Coefficient 12%
Bucket 2: BI from EUR 1 to 30 bn     Coefficient 15%
Bucket 3: BI above EUR 30 bn         Coefficient 18%
```

RBI's directions specify rupee equivalents of these thresholds.

The internal loss multiplier scales capital by loss history:

```
LC = 15 x Average annual operational loss over the past 10 years

ILM = ln( e - 1 + (LC / BIC)^0.8 )

ORC = BIC x ILM

Operational risk RWA = 12.5 x ORC
```

where `e - 1 = 1.71828`. The ILM equals 1 when LC equals BIC, rises above 1 for loss-heavy institutions, and falls below 1 for loss-light ones. Supervisors have national discretion to set ILM equal to 1 for all banks, removing the loss-history sensitivity entirely.

RBI finalised its Master Direction on Minimum Capital Requirements for Operational Risk in June 2023, with the effective date to be notified separately, and has announced revised Basel III capital adequacy norms for commercial banks from 1 April 2027. The applicable regime and the ILM treatment should be checked against the current direction.

## Worked Example

Example: a retail-focused bank with the same balance sheet scale as the NBFC used throughout - ₹4,450 crore of interest-earning loan assets, interest income ₹572 crore, interest expense ₹347 crore, other operating income ₹38 crore, other operating expense ₹22 crore, fee income ₹64 crore, fee expense ₹19 crore, trading book P&L ₹4 crore, banking book P&L ₹1 crore, and average annual operational loss of ₹16.08 crore.

Business indicator:

```
Net interest income      = ₹572 cr - ₹347 cr = ₹225 cr
Cap                      = 2.25% x ₹4,450 cr = ₹100.1 cr
ILDC = min(225, 100.1)   = ₹100.1 cr        <- the cap binds

SC = max(38, 22) + max(64, 19) = ₹38 cr + ₹64 cr = ₹102.0 cr

FC = |₹4 cr| + |₹1 cr| = ₹5.0 cr

BI = ₹100.1 cr + ₹102.0 cr + ₹5.0 cr = ₹207.1 cr
```

The cap removes ₹124.9 crore from the business indicator. For a lender running a 5.06% net interest margin, more than half the interest component is disregarded.

Business indicator component - BI sits well inside bucket 1:

```
BIC = 12% x ₹207.1 cr = ₹24.85 cr
```

Internal loss multiplier:

```
LC = 15 x ₹16.08 cr = ₹241.2 cr
LC / BIC = 241.2 / 24.85 = 9.706
(9.706)^0.8 = 6.162
ILM = ln(1.71828 + 6.162) = ln(7.880) = 2.064
```

Capital and RWA:

```
ORC = ₹24.85 cr x 2.064 = ₹51.30 cr
Operational risk RWA = 12.5 x ₹51.30 cr = ₹641 cr
```

Placed alongside the other risk types from Modules 1 and 2:

| Risk type | RWA | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Credit risk | ₹3,605 cr | 82.4% |
| Operational risk | ₹641 cr | 14.7% |
| Market risk | ₹130 cr | 3.0% |
| Total | ₹4,376 cr | 100% |

Operational risk is nearly five times market risk for this institution, driven almost entirely by the ILM of 2.064. The loss history of ₹16.08 crore a year - 0.36% of the book, which looked modest in 4.1 - is large relative to a business indicator suppressed by the interest cap, and the multiplier doubles the charge.

Sensitivity. Cutting annual operational losses to ₹9 crore, achievable largely by closing the fraud gap quantified in 4.2:

```
LC = 15 x ₹9 cr = ₹135 cr
LC / BIC = 5.433,  (5.433)^0.8 = 3.833
ILM = ln(1.71828 + 3.833) = ln(5.551) = 1.714
ORC = ₹24.85 cr x 1.714 = ₹42.60 cr
RWA = ₹532 cr
```

A ₹7.08 crore reduction in annual losses releases ₹109 crore of RWA - roughly ₹17 crore of capital at a 15.5% ratio, on top of the loss reduction itself. Where a supervisor sets ILM to 1, none of this sensitivity exists and ORC is ₹24.85 crore regardless of loss history.

## How it connects

The loss data driving the ILM is the profile compiled in 4.1, which is why loss data collection standards, boundary tagging, and near-miss capture have a direct capital consequence. The credit and market RWA figures come from 1.10 and 2.4. The forward-looking counterpart to this backward-looking capital measure is the RCSA process of 4.5, which assesses risks that have not yet produced losses.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Assuming operational risk capital scales with size alone. The ILM makes it scale with loss history, so two banks with identical balance sheets can carry very different charges.
- Overlooking the 2.25% ILDC cap. High-margin retail lenders are systematically advantaged by it, and models built on gross net interest income will overstate the charge.
- Treating the loss database as a compliance archive. It is a capital input. Under-reporting suppresses capital today and produces a step change when a supervisor requires completeness.
- Expecting AMA-style credit for good controls. The revised framework recognises loss experience, not control quality. A bank with excellent controls and a bad decade still carries a high ILM.
- Reading operational risk RWA as small by analogy with market risk. For a retail lender with a capped interest component, it is routinely the second-largest capital consumer.
