# 4.6 Key Risk Indicators

## Definition

A **key risk indicator (KRI)** is a metric that signals a change in risk exposure early enough to act on it. KRIs are distinguished from two neighbours that are often confused with them. A key performance indicator measures whether an objective is being achieved; a key control indicator measures whether a specific control is operating. A KRI measures whether the underlying risk is increasing - ideally before either performance or control metrics have moved.

## Why it matters

Operational losses in a servicing and collections operation are usually preceded by observable deterioration in inputs. Collections agent attrition rises, training quality falls, right-party contact rates decline, roll rates worsen, complaints increase, and eventually a conduct incident or a mass reconciliation failure produces a recorded loss. Each of those steps is measurable weeks or months before the loss. A KRI framework converts that sequence into a monitored dashboard with defined thresholds and named owners, so that escalation happens on a number rather than on someone's judgement that things feel wrong.

## Formula

Design criteria for a usable KRI:

Causally linked - there is a mechanism connecting the indicator to the risk, not just a historical correlation.

Leading - it moves before the loss, not with it. Delinquency is a lagging indicator; mandate failure rate is a leading one.

Measurable at frequency - available at least monthly, ideally weekly, from a system rather than a manual return.

Thresholded - green, amber, and red bands with a defined action at each, an accountable owner, and an escalation path.

Actionable - someone can do something about it. An indicator nobody can influence is a report, not a KRI.

Threshold setting uses one of two methods:

```
Statistical:  Amber = 75th percentile of the trailing 24-month distribution
              Red   = 90th percentile

Loss-based:   Red = the indicator value at which modelled downstream loss
              exceeds the risk appetite allocation for that process
```

The loss-based method is stronger where the transmission mechanism can be quantified, which in a collections operation it usually can.

## Worked Example

Example: KRI dashboard for the servicing and collections operation at the NBFC, current month.

| KRI | Current | Green | Amber | Red | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NACH mandate failure rate | 5.80% | < 4.50% | 4.50-6.00% | > 6.00% | Amber |
| Collections agent monthly attrition | 9.40% | < 6.00% | 6.00-9.00% | > 9.00% | Red |
| Right-party contact rate, buckets 1-2 | 58.10% | > 62.00% | 52.00-62.00% | < 52.00% | Amber |
| Recovery conduct complaints per 1,000 allocated accounts | 1.70 | < 0.80 | 0.80-1.50 | > 1.50 | Red |
| Customer complaints per 10,000 live accounts | 14.20 | < 11.00 | 11.00-18.00 | > 18.00 | Amber |
| Unreconciled collections cash aged over 3 days | ₹71 lakh | < ₹25 lakh | ₹25-60 lakh | > ₹60 lakh | Red |
| Payment posting errors per 100,000 transactions | 38 | < 30 | 30-55 | > 55 | Amber |
| Fraud queue backlog, average age | 4.1 days | < 1.0 day | 1.0-2.5 days | > 2.5 days | Red |

The dashboard is read as a chain, not as eight independent lines. Agent attrition at 9.4% has depleted experienced capacity, which has pushed right-party contact from a historical 64.0% down to 58.1%, which has weakened early-bucket collections, which shows up in roll rates.

Quantifying the transmission, using the roll-rate structure from 1.9:

```
Baseline:  Bucket 1 forward roll = 31.0%
Current:   Bucket 1 forward roll = 34.2%

Baseline chained flow to default
  = 0.0240 x 0.3100 x 0.5850 x 0.7600 = 0.331% per month
Current chained flow
  = 0.0240 x 0.3420 x 0.5850 x 0.7600 = 0.365% per month

Annualised flow to 90+:  3.97% -> 4.38%
Implied annual loss at 72% LGD:  2.86% -> 3.15%
Incremental loss on ₹1,840 cr = 0.29% x ₹1,840 cr = ₹5.3 cr per year
```

The attrition KRI is worth ₹5.3 crore a year in credit loss, which is what justifies a retention intervention costing far less. Setting the red threshold by the loss-based method: if the risk appetite allocation for collections execution is ₹2.5 crore of incremental loss, the corresponding roll-rate increase is 1.5 percentage points, which back-solves to an RPC floor of about 61% and an attrition ceiling of about 7.2% - tighter than the current statistical thresholds.

Two of the red lines are conduct and control rather than credit. Recovery conduct complaints at 1.7 per 1,000 allocated accounts is a supervisory exposure under RBI's fair practices requirements, and it correlates with agent inexperience from the same attrition driver. Unreconciled collections cash of ₹71 lakh aged over three days is an internal fraud exposure - the conditions in which cash misappropriation occurs - and links to the internal fraud line in 4.1.

## How it connects

The roll rates that transmit collections deterioration into loss are from 1.9, and the collections strategy the KRIs are measuring is from 1.12. Recovery conduct complaints tie to the fair practices requirements discussed there. Deterioration flagged by KRIs eventually appears as recorded losses in the profile of 4.1 and, with a long lag, in the capital charge of 4.4.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Using lagging metrics as KRIs. Delinquency, loss, and complaint volumes measure what has already happened. They are outcomes, and the point of a KRI is to move first.
- Setting thresholds from the statistical distribution alone. Percentile-based bands describe what is normal for the operation, not what is tolerable given the loss it produces. Where transmission can be quantified, the loss-based method should override.
- Building too many. A dashboard of forty indicators is not monitored; it is filed. Eight to fifteen per major process, each with an owner, is the workable range.
- Reading indicators independently. The value in the example above came from tracing attrition through contact rate to roll rate to rupees, which no single line shows.
- Leaving thresholds unrevised. As the operation changes - new channels, new products, automation - the historical distribution that set the bands stops describing the current process.
