# 4.5 Risk and Control Self-Assessment

## Definition

**Risk and control self-assessment (RCSA)** is a structured process in which the business unit that owns a process identifies the risks within it, assesses how large those risks would be without controls, evaluates the controls actually in place, and derives the residual risk that remains. The output is a documented risk profile with agreed remediation actions and named owners. It is a first-line activity - performed by the business, facilitated and challenged by the risk function - which is what distinguishes it from an audit.

## Why it matters

Loss data is backward-looking and sparse. A retail lender may have no recorded loss from a control that is about to fail, and the Basel capital calculation in 4.4 will reflect that silence as good news. RCSA is the forward-looking counterweight: it surfaces the risks that exist in the process design regardless of whether they have crystallised yet. It also produces something loss data cannot - an explicit link between a risk and the specific control meant to mitigate it, which is what makes remediation actionable. Where a control is rated weak and the residual risk exceeds appetite, the assessment has generated a decision rather than an observation.

## Formula

The process runs in six steps.

Scope definition - decompose the business into processes at a consistent level of granularity. For retail lending: sourcing, onboarding, underwriting, disbursement, servicing, collections, recovery, closure.

Inherent risk assessment - score each identified risk on likelihood and impact before considering controls, typically on 1-5 scales:

```
Inherent risk score = Likelihood x Impact
```

Control identification and rating - for each risk, list the controls, classify each as preventive or detective, manual or automated, and rate its effectiveness. A common convention converts the rating to a multiplier:

```
Strong    -> 0.3
Adequate  -> 0.6
Weak      -> 0.9

Residual risk score = Inherent risk score x Control effectiveness factor
```

Comparison against appetite - residual scores above the board-set threshold require an action plan with an owner and a date.

Action tracking - open items are tracked to closure and re-rated.

Attestation - the process owner signs the assessment, which is what makes it a self-assessment rather than a review.

Cadence is normally annual, with event-triggered refresh after a material loss, a process change, a system implementation, or a regulatory change.

## Worked Example

Example: RCSA of the disbursement process at the NBFC, with a board risk appetite threshold of 6.0 on the residual scale.

| Risk | L | I | Inherent | Key controls | Rating | Factor | Residual | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disbursement to an incorrect bank account | 3 | 4 | 12 | Penny-drop verification; maker-checker on account change | Adequate | 0.6 | 7.2 | Action required |
| Disbursement before completion of security creation (home loan) | 2 | 5 | 10 | Legal clearance checklist; system block pending charge registration | Strong | 0.3 | 3.0 | Within appetite |
| Incorrect interest rate or tenor keyed at booking | 4 | 3 | 12 | Automated pull from approved sanction; exception report | Adequate | 0.6 | 7.2 | Action required |
| Duplicate disbursement on the same sanction | 2 | 4 | 8 | System-enforced one-time disbursement flag | Strong | 0.3 | 2.4 | Within appetite |
| Disbursement on an application with unresolved fraud flag | 3 | 5 | 15 | Manual queue clearance by fraud unit | Weak | 0.9 | 13.5 | Action required |
| Key Fact Statement not issued or incomplete | 3 | 4 | 12 | Template auto-generation; sampling by compliance | Adequate | 0.6 | 7.2 | Action required |

Worked line for the fraud flag risk:

```
Inherent = Likelihood 3 x Impact 5 = 15
Control rated Weak: the fraud queue is cleared manually, has no
system block, and had a 4-day average backlog in the review period
Residual = 15 x 0.9 = 13.5, against an appetite threshold of 6.0
```

This is the highest residual score in the process and connects directly to the 240 fraud accounts booked in 4.1. The agreed action is a hard system block preventing disbursement while a fraud flag is open, which converts the control from weak-manual to strong-automated and takes the residual score to 4.5.

Quantifying the top action. If the manual queue is responsible for even 15% of the 240 booked frauds:

```
36 accounts x ₹3,92,000 x 96% = ₹1.35 cr per year of avoidable loss
```

Which also feeds the ILM reduction modelled in 4.4.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Rating controls on design rather than operation. A well-designed control with a four-day processing backlog is operating weakly regardless of how it is documented.
- Scoring inherent risk after mentally netting off controls. The inherent score must assume no controls, or the residual calculation double counts them and everything lands within appetite.
- Producing a heat map and stopping. The output that matters is the action list with owners and dates; the map is a presentation artefact.
- Allowing the risk function to write the assessment. If the business does not own it, it is an audit with a different name and will not change behaviour.
- Running it annually and never in between. Process changes, system releases, and channel additions all invalidate prior assessments, and the gap between cycles is where new risk accumulates unrecorded.
