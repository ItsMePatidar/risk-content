# 4.3 KYC and Onboarding Controls

## Definition

Know Your Customer (KYC) is the regulatory process of establishing and verifying a customer's identity and address, assessing their risk profile, and maintaining that information over the relationship. In India it is governed by RBI's Master Direction on KYC, which operationalises obligations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act. Customer due diligence can be performed face-to-face using officially valid documents, through Aadhaar-based electronic verification, or through **video-based customer identification process (V-CIP)**, a remote alternative with prescribed technical and procedural safeguards. Onboarding controls sit alongside KYC and serve a different master: KYC answers whether the lender may deal with this person, onboarding controls answer whether the application is genuine.

## Why it matters

Onboarding is the only point in the lifecycle where the lender controls whether an exposure is created at all. Every downstream control - collections, recovery, enforcement - operates on a loan that already exists. A control gap here converts directly into the fraud losses of 4.2, and into regulatory exposure that is separate from and additional to the credit loss. The digital lending model raises the stakes: applications arrive without a human ever meeting the applicant, sourcing runs through lending service providers, and the entire verification chain is technological. RBI's digital lending directions respond to this by requiring that the credit decision rest with the regulated entity rather than the LSP, mandating a Key Fact Statement with all-inclusive APR disclosure, prescribing a cooling-off period, restricting data collection and storage, and capping default loss guarantee arrangements.

## Formula

The onboarding control stack, in execution order:

Deduplication - matching the applicant against existing customers, prior rejects, and internal negative lists on name, date of birth, mobile, PAN, and Aadhaar reference. Weak dedupe logic is the most common single gap, because fuzzy name matching across Indian naming conventions is genuinely hard.

Identity verification - OVD validation, Aadhaar-based verification, or V-CIP. Note the constraint on OTP-based Aadhaar e-KYC in non-face-to-face mode: aggregate term loans sanctioned to a customer verified this way are subject to a prescribed ceiling, which forces higher-ticket lending into V-CIP or full CDD.

Address verification - the weakest link in most retail processes, since an OVD proves the address was valid when the document was issued, not that the applicant lives there now.

Screening - name matching against sanctions and watch lists, and politically exposed person identification.

Risk categorisation and CKYC - assigning a low, medium, or high risk category that determines periodic updation frequency, and uploading the record to the Central KYC Records Registry.

Fraud and integrity checks - the device, velocity, and document integrity layers described in 4.2.

The control effectiveness measure:

```
Detection rate = Fraud stopped pre-disbursement / (Fraud stopped + Fraud booked)

Marginal value of 1 pp detection improvement
    = Total fraud attempts x 1% x Average loss per booked fraud
```

## Worked Example

Example: onboarding control performance at the NBFC, on 88,920 personal loan applications per year.

Control funnel outcomes:

| Control | Applications flagged | Confirmed fraud among flagged | False positive ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deduplication | 2,616 | 118 | 21.2 : 1 |
| Device and velocity rules | 1,842 | 294 | 5.3 : 1 |
| Document integrity check | 964 | 186 | 4.2 : 1 |
| Employer and income verification | 731 | 42 | 16.4 : 1 |
| Name screening | 372 | 0 | not a fraud control |

Detection performance:

```
Fraud stopped pre-disbursement = 640
Fraud booked (from 4.1)        = 240
Total fraud attempts           = 880

Detection rate = 640 / 880 = 72.7%
```

Marginal value of improvement:

```
1 pp improvement = 880 x 1% = 8.8 fewer fraud accounts booked
Value = 8.8 x ₹3,92,000 x 96% LGD = ₹33.1 lakh per year per percentage point
```

That figure is the budget test for any proposed onboarding control. A tool costing ₹80 lakh a year must deliver more than 2.4 percentage points of detection improvement to justify itself.

Gap analysis from the same data. Device and velocity rules have the best yield at a 5.3:1 false positive ratio, and deduplication the worst at 21.2:1 - 2,616 applications sent to manual review to find 118 frauds. At an estimated ₹210 per manual review, dedupe review costs ₹5.49 lakh a year to prevent ₹4.44 crore of loss, so it remains strongly positive, but the false positive rate is a customer experience cost as well as a review cost: 2,498 genuine applicants were delayed. Tightening the fuzzy match threshold would cut false positives and lose some detections, and the trade should be evaluated on the ₹33.1 lakh per percentage point figure above rather than on review cost alone.

## How it connects

These controls are the prevention layer for the fraud losses quantified in 4.2 and recorded in the loss profile of 4.1. The dedupe and verification steps sit inside the underwriting waterfall of 1.7, before the scorecard decision. Control design and effectiveness ratings for this process are what an RCSA (4.5) formally assesses, and the flagged-application volumes and false positive ratios are natural KRIs (4.6). Where V-CIP or e-KYC is used, the failure and retry rates are operational metrics with direct fraud consequences.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Treating KYC compliance as fraud prevention. They overlap but are not the same. A fully KYC-compliant onboarding can approve a first-party fraudster whose identity is entirely genuine.
- Relying on OVDs for current address. The document proves issuance, not residence. Independent address verification - geotagged capture, utility data, delivery confirmation - is a separate control.
- Underestimating V-CIP attack surface. Remote video verification is exposed to injected video streams and synthetic media, and requires liveness detection and randomised challenge steps rather than a recorded session review.
- Accepting LSP-performed verification uncritically. The regulated entity retains responsibility for the credit decision and for KYC compliance regardless of who operates the front end.
- Measuring controls by flag volume. A control that flags a large number of applications is not thereby effective; the false positive ratio and the incremental detections it produces over the other controls are what matter.
