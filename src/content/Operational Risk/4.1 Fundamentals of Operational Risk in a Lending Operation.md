# 4.1 Fundamentals of Operational Risk in a Lending Operation

## Definition

**Operational risk** is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. The Basel definition includes legal risk - fines, penalties, and settlements arising from supervisory action or litigation - and excludes strategic and reputational risk. In a retail lending business it is the risk category that touches the largest number of people and transactions: every application verified, every disbursement released, every EMI posted, every collections call made is an operational event with a failure mode.

## Why it matters

Retail lending is a volume business, and volume converts small per-transaction failure rates into large annual losses. A 0.3% error rate on payment posting is invisible in any single month and produces thousands of misapplied payments a year, each generating a customer complaint, a manual correction, and occasionally an incorrect bureau report that the lender must remediate. The loss profile has two distinct halves that require different management: high-frequency, low-severity process failures that are managed through control design and automation, and low-frequency, high-severity events - a systemic interest miscalculation across a product, a data breach, a supervisory penalty for recovery agent conduct - that are managed through governance and scenario analysis. Optimising only for the first half leaves the second unmanaged, and the second is what ends careers.

## Formula

Annual expected operational loss is the product of frequency and severity, summed across event types:

```
Expected annual loss = SUM over event types of ( Frequency_i x Average severity_i )
```

where:
- `Frequency_i` = number of loss events of type i per year
- `Average severity_i` = mean gross loss per event of type i, in ₹, before recoveries and insurance

The Basel event-type taxonomy, mapped to a retail lending operation:

| Basel event type | Retail lending manifestation |
|---|---|
| Internal fraud | Staff or DSA collusion, fictitious accounts, cash misappropriation in collections |
| External fraud | Application and identity fraud, forged documents, card transaction fraud |
| Employment practices and workplace safety | Employment disputes, field collections staff safety |
| Clients, products and business practices | Mis-selling, unfair recovery practices, mis-disclosure of APR, ombudsman awards |
| Damage to physical assets | Branch and record storage losses |
| Business disruption and system failures | LOS or LMS outage, payment gateway failure, mandate processing failure |
| Execution, delivery and process management | Incorrect interest computation, wrong disbursement, foreclosure calculation errors, bureau reporting errors |

Loss data conventions that matter: **gross loss** is the amount before recoveries and insurance; **net loss** is after. A **near miss** is a control failure that did not result in loss, and it is the most informative and least reported category. **Boundary events** - losses that arise from an operational failure but crystallise as credit losses - must be tagged, or the credit function will be blamed for fraud.

## Worked Example

Example: annual operational loss profile for the NBFC from Module 3 - ₹4,450 crore book, roughly 46,500 live accounts, 7,410 personal loan applications and 2,520 bookings per month.

| Event type | Events per year | Average severity | Total loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| External fraud (application and identity) | 240 | ₹3,92,000 | ₹9.41 cr |
| Execution, delivery and process management | 3,180 | ₹9,400 | ₹2.99 cr |
| Clients, products and business practices | 68 | ₹2,40,000 | ₹1.63 cr |
| Internal fraud | 11 | ₹8,60,000 | ₹0.95 cr |
| Business disruption and system failures | 6 | ₹14,20,000 | ₹0.85 cr |
| Employment practices | 9 | ₹1,80,000 | ₹0.16 cr |
| Damage to physical assets | 3 | ₹3,10,000 | ₹0.09 cr |
| Total | 3,517 | | ₹16.08 cr |

The distribution is the point:

```
Execution events   = 3,180 / 3,517 = 90.4% of events, but only 18.6% of loss
External fraud     =   240 / 3,517 =  6.8% of events, and     58.5% of loss
Total loss / book  = ₹16.08 cr / ₹4,450 cr = 0.36%
```

Against a credit cost of 2.95% on the personal loan book, operational loss is roughly an eighth the size. That proportion is normal and is a useful check on where risk management attention should sit. But note two things it conceals. First, the ₹9.41 crore of fraud loss is almost certainly sitting inside the credit loss line as well, because fraudulent accounts default and get written off as credit - the same rupee counted once in the credit budget and once here, unless boundary tagging is enforced. Second, the table has no entry for the tail. A single supervisory penalty for digital lending non-compliance, or a mass remediation of incorrectly computed foreclosure charges across 8,000 accounts, would exceed the entire annual profile.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Leaving boundary events untagged. Fraud losses booked as credit write-offs corrupt both the operational loss database and the PD model's development sample, since a fraudulent account is not a credit event and should be excluded from scorecard training data.
- Managing to the frequency table. The events that matter for capital and survival are in the tail, and the tail by definition has few or no entries in a three-year loss history.
- Ignoring near misses. A payment file that was corrupted and caught before release is the same control failure as one that was not caught; only the outcome differed.
- Treating conduct issues as reputational rather than operational. Mis-selling and recovery conduct produce quantifiable losses - refunds, ombudsman awards, penalties - and sit squarely inside the Basel definition.
