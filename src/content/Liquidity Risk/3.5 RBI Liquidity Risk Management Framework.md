# 3.5 RBI Liquidity Risk Management Framework

## Definition

RBI regulates liquidity through separate frameworks for banks and for NBFCs, applying broadly similar tools with different thresholds and perimeters. Banks are subject to the ALM structural liquidity statement with prescribed bucket tolerance limits, the Liquidity Coverage Ratio, and the Net Stable Funding Ratio. NBFCs are governed by the Liquidity Risk Management Framework issued in November 2019 and subsequently incorporated into the Scale Based Regulation master direction, which introduced granular near-term buckets, an LCR requirement for larger entities, a set of liquidity monitoring tools and stock ratios, and mandatory contingency funding plans.

## Why it matters

The 2019 framework was a direct response to the NBFC funding crisis, and its design reflects the specific failure mode observed: entities funding long retail assets with very short wholesale money, with no buffer and no plan. The structural point behind all of it is that NBFCs have no access to the central bank as lender of last resort. A bank facing a funding shock can borrow from the RBI against government securities through the liquidity adjustment facility; an NBFC cannot. That single asymmetry explains why the NBFC framework is prescriptive about buffers and buckets in a way the bank framework does not need to be.

## Formula

For NBFCs, the framework has four components.

Granular ALM buckets - the 1-30 day bucket is split into 1-7 days, 8-14 days, and 15-30 days, with cumulative negative mismatch capped at 10%, 10%, and 20% of cumulative cash outflows respectively.

Liquidity Coverage Ratio - applicable to deposit-taking NBFCs and to non-deposit-taking NBFCs above prescribed asset-size thresholds, phased to a 100% minimum, with the stress convention described in 3.3.

Liquidity risk monitoring tools - concentration of funding by counterparty, by instrument, and by currency; available unencumbered assets; and a set of stock ratios:

```
Commercial paper as % of total public funds, total liabilities, and total assets
NCDs of original maturity under 1 year, as % of the same three denominators
Short-term liabilities as % of total public funds, total liabilities, and total assets
Long-term assets to long-term liabilities
```

Contingency funding plan - a board-approved document identifying stress triggers, escalation authority, and the specific funding actions available, with execution timelines.

Governance requirements sit alongside: a board-level risk management committee, an ALCO responsible for day-to-day management, a designated Chief Risk Officer for larger NBFCs, and public disclosure of LCR and funding concentration.

For banks, the parallel structure is the ALM guidelines with negative gap tolerances on near-term buckets, LCR at a 100% minimum, and NSFR - implemented in India from 1 October 2021 - which requires available stable funding to exceed required stable funding over a one-year horizon. The revised LCR guidelines effective 1 April 2026 added the 2.5 percentage point run-off overlay on internet and mobile banking enabled retail and small-business deposits, and reduced the run-off rate on funding from non-financial entities such as trusts, partnerships, and LLPs to 40%.

## Worked Example

Example: a compliance dashboard for the NBFC from 3.1, with total assets of ₹5,150 crore (₹4,450 crore loans, ₹556 crore liquid assets, ₹144 crore other), total liabilities of ₹4,530 crore, and borrowings - public funds - of ₹4,010 crore.

Stock ratios:

```
CP as % of total public funds       = ₹350 cr / ₹4,010 cr   =  8.73%
CP as % of total assets             = ₹350 cr / ₹5,150 cr   =  6.80%
Short-term liabilities (<= 1 yr)    = ₹2,805 cr
  as % of total public funds        = ₹2,805 cr / ₹4,010 cr = 69.95%
  as % of total liabilities         = ₹2,805 cr / ₹4,530 cr = 61.92%
Long-term assets (> 1 yr)           = ₹3,180 cr
Long-term liabilities (> 1 yr)      = ₹1,205 cr
  Long-term assets / liabilities    = 3,180 / 1,205         =  2.64x
```

Compliance position:

| Metric | Value | Requirement or internal limit | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| LCR | 71.7% | 100% | Breach |
| Cumulative mismatch, 1-7 days (with buffer) | +452.5% | Not below -10% | Compliant |
| Cumulative mismatch, 15-30 days (with buffer) | +8.9% | Not below -20% | Compliant |
| CP / total public funds | 8.73% | Internal cap 15% | Compliant |
| Short-term liabilities / total liabilities | 61.92% | Internal cap 55% | Breach |
| Long-term assets / long-term liabilities | 2.64x | Internal cap 2.00x | Breach |

Reading the dashboard together rather than line by line: the CP ratio is comfortably within its cap, which is why a funding-concentration view alone would have found nothing. The problem is the maturity profile of the whole liability stack - nearly 70% of public funds mature within a year against 71% of assets that do not. The ALM buckets pass only because the liquid asset buffer is counted as a day-one inflow, and the LCR, which stresses that buffer against stressed outflows, is the metric that catches it. Three of the six lines flag the same underlying condition through different lenses, which is the intended design of the monitoring set.

## How it connects

The bucket limits applied here are the ones used to construct the structural liquidity statement in 3.2, and the LCR line is the calculation from 3.3. The contingency funding plan requirement is what the stress test in 3.4 exists to validate, and the survival horizon of 33 days is the number that should sit alongside this dashboard at the board risk committee. The absence of lender-of-last-resort access for NBFCs is the reason the funding structure analysed in 3.1 carries more risk in an NBFC than the same structure would in a bank.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Reading stock ratios individually. Each is a partial view; the condition they are collectively designed to detect is maturity transformation beyond the buffer's ability to absorb it.
- Assuming LCR compliance implies ALM compliance, or the reverse. They apply different stress assumptions to the same balance sheet and routinely disagree, as the dashboard above shows.
- Treating the contingency funding plan as a document. Its value is entirely in whether the actions listed can be executed within the stated timelines, which requires standing relationships and pre-positioned documentation, not a policy file.
- Overlooking NSFR for banks. LCR addresses 30 days; NSFR addresses the structural one-year mismatch, and a lender can satisfy the first while failing the second.
- Applying bank frameworks to NBFCs unmodified. The run-off assumptions, the perimeter, the thresholds, and above all the availability of central bank liquidity are different, and the last of these is not a parameter that can be adjusted for.
