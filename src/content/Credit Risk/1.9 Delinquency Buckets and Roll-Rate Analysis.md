# 1.9 Delinquency Buckets and Roll-Rate Analysis

## Definition

Retail lenders classify accounts by days past due into discrete buckets: Current (0 DPD), Bucket 1 (1-30 DPD), Bucket 2 (31-60), Bucket 3 (61-90), and 90+ DPD, which is normally the default and NPA trigger. A **roll rate** is the proportion of a bucket that moves into the next worse bucket over one month; a **roll-rate matrix** is the full set of month-to-month transition probabilities across all buckets, including back-rolls (accounts that pay up and improve) and cures. Chaining the sequential forward roll rates gives the share of currently performing balance expected to reach default.

## Why it matters

Roll rates are the highest-frequency credit signal a retail lender has. The 90+ ratio reports a decision the borrower made four months ago; the Current-to-Bucket-1 roll rate reports what happened last month. Because the chain is multiplicative, a deterioration in the earliest link propagates through the whole structure, so the front-bucket roll rate is a genuine leading indicator with roughly a one-quarter lead on the default number. Roll rates are also the operational control surface for collections: each bucket has its own contact strategy, cost, and success rate, and the roll rate is how their effectiveness is measured. Under IFRS9, transition matrices are one of the standard mechanisms for constructing lifetime PD from short-horizon observations.

## Formula

```
Roll rate (Bi -> Bi+1) = Balance in bucket Bi+1 at month t+1 / Balance in bucket Bi at month t

Flow to default (monthly) = R(C->B1) x R(B1->B2) x R(B2->B3) x R(B3->90+)

Implied annual loss rate = Flow to default (monthly) x 12 x LGD
```

where:
- `R(Bi -> Bi+1)` = forward roll rate between adjacent buckets, in % per month
- `Balance` = outstanding principal, not account count; balance weighting is the correct basis because loss is denominated in rupees
- `C` = Current bucket
- `LGD` = loss given default, in %

The complement of the forward roll is not simply "stays put" - accounts either roll forward, hold, or roll backward:

```
R(forward) + R(hold) + R(backward) = 100%
```

The backward component is the **cure rate**, and it is the direct measure of collections effectiveness in early buckets. A Bucket 1 cure rate below about 60% on an unsecured book indicates a contact-rate or capacity problem, not a borrower quality problem.

The annualisation above is a linear approximation. It assumes the monthly flow rate persists and ignores portfolio amortisation, so it overstates for short-tenor products. It is used for monitoring, not for provisioning.

## Worked Example

Example: monthly balance-weighted roll rates on an unsecured personal loan book.

| Transition | Roll rate |
|---|---|
| Current -> 1-30 DPD | 2.40% |
| 1-30 -> 31-60 DPD | 31.00% |
| 31-60 -> 61-90 DPD | 58.50% |
| 61-90 -> 90+ DPD | 76.00% |

Chained flow from performing to default:

```
Flow = 0.0240 x 0.3100 x 0.5850 x 0.7600 = 0.003308 = 0.331% per month

Annualised flow to 90+ = 0.331% x 12 = 3.97% of performing balance
Implied annual loss    = 3.97% x 72% LGD = 2.86%
```

This reconciles closely with the 2.95% expected loss computed bottom-up in 1.5 from PD 4.10% x LGD 72%. When roll-rate implied loss and parameter-based EL diverge by more than 20-30 bps, one of the two is wrong and the reconciliation is worth doing before either number is used for pricing.

Early warning. Suppose next month's Current-to-Bucket-1 roll comes in at 3.10% while the three deeper roll rates are unchanged:

```
Flow = 0.0310 x 0.3100 x 0.5850 x 0.7600 = 0.004273 = 0.427% per month
Annualised flow = 5.13%
Implied annual loss = 5.13% x 72% = 3.69%
```

An 83 bps increase in expected loss, detected from a single front-bucket movement, roughly three months before the 90+ ratio registers anything. On a ₹1,840 crore book that is ₹15.3 crore of annualised loss becoming visible one quarter early - which is the entire practical value of the technique.

Diagnosing where to intervene. Sensitivity of the chained flow to a 10% relative improvement in each individual roll rate is identical in percentage terms, because the chain is multiplicative. The choice therefore comes down to cost and feasibility. Improving the Bucket 1 roll from 31.0% to 27.9% requires more early-stage calling capacity at perhaps ₹90 per contact; improving the Bucket 3 roll from 76.0% to 68.4% requires field visits and settlement authority at ₹1,400 per account, on a far smaller population. Front-bucket intervention is almost always the cheaper rupee of loss avoided, which is the analytical basis for the bucket-wise strategies in 1.12.

## How it connects

Roll rates are the monthly-frequency counterpart to vintage analysis (1.8): vintages identify the bad cohort, roll rates show what that cohort is doing right now. The chained flow provides an independent check on the PD estimate from 1.2 and the EL from 1.5. Bucket definitions map directly onto IFRS9 staging (1.11), where 30+ DPD is the rebuttable presumption for Stage 2 and 90+ for Stage 3, and onto RBI IRAC classification (1.15). Bucket-level roll rates are the performance metric for every collections strategy in 1.12.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Computing roll rates on account counts. Loss is measured in rupees, and large accounts roll differently from small ones. Account-weighted matrices are useful for capacity planning and misleading for loss forecasting.
- Ignoring cures and back-rolls. A book with a 31% forward roll from Bucket 1 and a 68% cure rate is in a very different state from one with a 31% forward roll and a 45% cure rate plus 24% holding. The forward number alone does not distinguish them.
- Reading month-to-month movement as signal. Roll rates are strongly seasonal - salary credit dates, festival months, and agricultural cash cycles all move them. Compare against the same month in prior years, or use a three-month moving average, before declaring a trend.
- Letting restructured accounts sit in Current. An account restructured out of Bucket 3 exits the delinquency data entirely and suppresses every downstream roll rate. Restructured exposure must be tracked as its own pool with its own roll matrix.
- Treating the annualised flow as a provisioning number. It is a monitoring approximation that ignores amortisation, cures after 90+, and the discounting that IFRS9 requires.
