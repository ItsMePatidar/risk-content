# 1.1 Fundamentals of Credit Risk - The Lending Lifecycle
 
## Definition
 
Credit risk in retail lending is the risk that a borrower stops paying as contracted, and the lender recovers less than the amount owed. It is measured at the account level but managed at the portfolio level, because no single personal loan or credit card matters on its own. Formally, **credit risk** is the expected and unexpected loss arising from obligor default or credit deterioration over a defined horizon. The lending lifecycle is the sequence of decisions - origination, underwriting, servicing, collections - where that risk is created, priced, monitored, and finally realised or mitigated.
 
## Why it matters
 
Retail loan books are thin-margin, high-volume businesses. A personal loan portfolio might earn 8-9% net interest margin against a 3% credit cost, so a 100 bps increase in loss rate destroys roughly a third of the profit. Losses are also lagged: a bad underwriting decision made in January shows up as a 90+ DPD account in the following January, by which time twelve more months of similar accounts have been booked. Regulators care because retail books are where consumer harm and systemic mispricing accumulate quietly - the RBI monitors sectoral credit growth and has raised risk weights on unsecured consumer credit precisely when origination outran underwriting discipline. If the lifecycle is treated as four disconnected departments rather than one feedback loop, the lender loses the ability to detect that its January cohort was mispriced until the capital is already gone.
 
## Formula
 
The lifecycle has four stages, each owning a specific lever:
 
Origination - sourcing and application capture. Lever: channel mix and lead quality.
Underwriting - the accept/decline/price decision. Lever: cutoffs, policy rules, limit assignment.
Servicing - the performing life of the account. Lever: behavioural monitoring, limit management, early warning.
Collections - action once the account misses payment. Lever: contact strategy, settlement, legal recovery.
 
The economics that tie the four stages together are captured in the account-level risk-adjusted return:
 
```
RAROA = NIM - OPEX - EL
 
NIM  = Asset Yield - Cost of Funds
EL   = PD x LGD x EAD (expressed as % of average assets)
```
 
where:
- `RAROA` = risk-adjusted return on assets, pre-tax, in % per annum
- `NIM` = net interest margin, the spread retained after funding cost, in % per annum
- `Asset Yield` = contractual interest plus fee income earned on the average outstanding balance, in % per annum
- `Cost of Funds` = blended borrowing cost allocated to the product, in % per annum
- `OPEX` = acquisition cost (amortised over the expected behavioural life of the account) plus annual servicing and collections cost, in % of average assets
- `EL` = expected credit loss for the year, in % of average assets
- `PD` = probability of default over a 12-month horizon, in %
- `LGD` = loss given default, the share of exposure not recovered, in %
- `EAD` = exposure at default, the balance outstanding when default occurs, in ₹
Acquisition cost per booked account is a function of funnel efficiency, not just marketing spend:
 
```
Acquisition cost per booked account = Cost per application / Approval rate
```
 
where:
- `Cost per application` = fully loaded sourcing cost including channel commission, bureau pull, and verification, in ₹
- `Approval rate` = booked accounts divided by applications received, in %
This is the first place origination and underwriting collide. Tightening the cutoff lowers PD but raises acquisition cost per booked account, because the same sourcing spend now yields fewer bookings.
 
## Worked Example
 
Example: A ₹4,80,000 unsecured personal loan, 36-month tenor, 16.5% per annum reducing balance.
 
EMI calculation:
 
```
EMI = P x r x (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1)
    = 4,80,000 x 0.01375 x 1.63489 / 0.63489
    = ₹16,996 per month
```
 
where `P` = ₹4,80,000 principal, `r` = 0.1650 / 12 = 1.375% monthly, `n` = 36 months.
 
Funnel and acquisition cost. The lender spends ₹1,650 per application (DSA commission accrual, bureau pull at ₹58, income verification, and field investigation). The approval rate is 34%.
 
```
Acquisition cost per booked account = ₹1,650 / 0.34 = ₹4,853
```
 
That is 1.01% of the ₹4,80,000 principal. Personal loans in this segment prepay, so the expected behavioural life is 23 months, not 36. Amortised: 1.01% x (12 / 23) = 0.53% per annum.
 
Account economics, expressed on average assets:
 
| Line | % p.a. |
|---|---|
| Asset yield (16.50% interest + 1.10% processing fee amortised) | 17.60% |
| Cost of funds | (7.90%) |
| NIM | 9.70% |
| Acquisition cost, amortised | (0.53%) |
| Servicing and collections cost | (1.85%) |
| Expected loss (PD 4.10% x LGD 72% x EAD 100%) | (2.95%) |
| RAROA, pre-tax | 4.37% |
 
Now break one stage. Suppose the DSA channel is expanded to hit a volume target, and the incremental accounts carry a PD of 6.80% instead of 4.10%. Expected loss on those accounts becomes 6.80% x 72% = 4.90%. RAROA falls to 2.42% - a 45% reduction in profitability, produced entirely at the origination stage, and visible in the P&L only 12 to 15 months later.
 
The collections lever works in the opposite direction. Improving LGD from 72% to 66% through better early-bucket contact rates cuts expected loss on the base case from 2.95% to 2.71%, adding 24 bps to RAROA without writing a single new loan.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions
 
- Treating credit risk as an underwriting-only discipline. Roughly a third of realised loss reduction in a mature retail book comes from servicing and collections execution, not from the accept/decline decision.
- Reading loss rates on a calendar basis. Total portfolio delinquency is a weighted average of cohorts at different ages, so a book growing at 40% per annum will show a falling delinquency percentage even as underwriting quality deteriorates. Only vintage-based measurement removes this dilution.
- Confusing approval rate with risk appetite. A falling approval rate can mean tighter policy or worse incoming lead quality. These require opposite management responses, and the approval rate alone cannot distinguish them.
- Assuming acquisition cost is a marketing line item. It is a credit variable, because it moves inversely with the cutoff and therefore constrains how much underwriting tightening is economically viable.
