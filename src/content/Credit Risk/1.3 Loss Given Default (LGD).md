# 1.3 Loss Given Default (LGD)

## Definition

**Loss given default (LGD)** is the share of exposure a lender ultimately fails to recover once an account has defaulted, expressed as a percentage of exposure at default. It is the mirror image of the recovery rate: LGD = 1 - RR. Two things make it harder to estimate than it looks - recoveries arrive over months or years and must be discounted back to the default date, and the costs of collecting must be netted off. In retail, LGD is driven by one of two mechanisms depending on the product: liquidating collateral (home loan, auto loan, LAP) or grinding out cash through collections effort (personal loan, credit card, BNPL).

## Why it matters

LGD is the term that separates secured from unsecured economics. A home loan with a 1.2% PD and a 22% LGD carries an expected loss of 26 bps; a personal loan with the same 1.2% PD and an 84% LGD carries 101 bps. That single input decides whether a product can be priced at 8.6% or needs 16.5%. LGD is also where recovery operations show up in the P&L, so it is the one credit parameter that a lender can move within a quarter rather than a cycle. Regulators scrutinise it because it is easy to flatter: exclude cured accounts, ignore collection costs, skip discounting, and a genuine 78% LGD can be reported as 55%.

## Formula

```
LGD = 1 - RR

RR = [ SUM( Rt / (1+d)^t ) - SUM( Ct / (1+d)^t ) ] / EAD
```

where:
- `RR` = recovery rate, in % of exposure at default
- `Rt` = cash recovered in period t, in ₹ - auction proceeds, settlement payments, insurance claims
- `Ct` = direct cost incurred in period t, in ₹ - repossession, yard storage, legal fees, agency commission
- `d` = discount rate per period; the effective interest rate on the contract is the standard choice under IFRS9
- `t` = number of periods between the default date and the cash flow
- `EAD` = exposure at default, in ₹

The workout period runs from the default date to the point where the account is closed, written off, or recoveries fall below a materiality threshold. For secured retail this is typically 9 to 30 months; for unsecured it can run past 48 months at very low incremental yield.

Three variants of LGD exist and are not interchangeable:

Workout LGD - computed from realised, discounted cash flows on closed accounts. This is the estimation basis.
Downturn LGD - workout LGD re-estimated under stressed conditions, principally lower collateral values and longer disposal timelines. Basel IRB requires it because collateral value and default frequency are correlated.
Cure-adjusted LGD - accounts that default and then return to performing status have near-zero loss. Including them lowers average LGD substantially, so the treatment of cures must match the default definition used in PD estimation.

## Worked Example

Example A - secured, collateral-driven. An auto loan defaults with an EAD of ₹5,62,400. The effective interest rate is 13.25% per annum (1.1042% per month).

| Cash flow | Timing from default | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Auction proceeds | Month 11 | ₹4,18,000 |
| Repossession and yard costs | Month 5 | (₹22,500) |
| Legal and notice costs | Month 6 | (₹8,400) |
| Auction commission at 2.5% | Month 11 | (₹10,450) |

Net nominal recovery: ₹4,18,000 - ₹22,500 - ₹8,400 - ₹10,450 = ₹3,76,650.

Discounting the net position at an average 11-month lag:

```
Discount factor = 1 / (1.011042)^11 = 1 / 1.1284 = 0.8862
PV of net recovery = ₹3,76,650 x 0.8862 = ₹3,33,790
RR = ₹3,33,790 / ₹5,62,400 = 59.35%
LGD = 1 - 0.5935 = 40.65%
```

Note how much of the loss is timing and friction rather than collateral shortfall. The vehicle fetched 74.3% of EAD at auction; costs and the 11-month delay consumed a further 15 percentage points.

Example B - unsecured, collections-driven. A personal loan defaults with an EAD of ₹3,18,700. Collections recover 26.0% gross over the workout, at an agency commission of 22% of cash collected, with an average recovery lag of 14 months and an EIR of 18.0% per annum (1.50% per month).

```
Gross recovery      = ₹3,18,700 x 26.0%  = ₹82,862
Agency commission   = ₹82,862 x 22%      = (₹18,230)
Net nominal         = ₹64,632
Discount factor     = 1 / (1.015)^14     = 0.8118
PV of net recovery  = ₹52,468
RR  = ₹52,468 / ₹3,18,700 = 16.46%
LGD = 83.54%
```

The same lender, the same collections team, the same discount discipline - and a 43 percentage point LGD gap driven entirely by the presence of a liquidatable asset.

## How it connects

LGD is the second term in the expected loss identity of 1.5 and, with PD (1.2), determines whether a product's pricing covers its risk. It is the parameter directly targeted by collections and recovery strategy (1.12) and by credit risk mitigation structures - collateral, guarantors, insurance (1.13). Under the Basel Standardized Approach (1.10) LGD is not estimated at all; it is embedded in prescribed risk weights, whereas Advanced IRB requires own estimates including downturn LGD. Under IFRS9 (1.11) LGD must be point-in-time and forward-looking, and discounted at the original EIR.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Reporting undiscounted recovery rates. Ignoring an 11-month lag at a 13.25% EIR overstated recovery by 6.8 percentage points in Example A. On a large secured book this is the difference between adequate and deficient provisioning.
- Netting collection costs inconsistently. Agency commission is almost always included; internal collections salaries, call-centre cost, and legal department overhead frequently are not. The resulting LGD understates true economic loss.
- Treating average LGD as representative. Retail LGD is bimodal, not normal - secured accounts cluster near full recovery or near-total loss depending on whether the asset was traced. The mean sits in a region where few accounts actually land, so segment-level LGD is required for pricing.
- Using recent-vintage LGD without a maturity adjustment. Accounts that defaulted 8 months ago are still recovering. Including them in the denominator of a completed-workout calculation biases LGD upward; excluding them entirely biases it downward by selecting for fast recoveries.
- Assuming collateral value equals LGD protection. In LAP and home loans, the binding constraint is enforceability and disposal time under SARFAESI, not the valuation on file.
