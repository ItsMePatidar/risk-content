# 1.12 Collections and Recovery Strategy

## Definition

Collections is the operational function that recovers money from accounts that have missed a payment, and it is the only credit lever that acts on losses already in motion. It is organised by delinquency bucket, because contact strategy, cost, and success probability all change sharply with depth of delinquency. **Recovery** refers to action after default - settlement, legal enforcement, asset repossession, agency placement, and eventually write-off. The distinction matters operationally: collections aims to cure, recovery aims to maximise cash on an account already lost to the performing book.

## Why it matters

Collections is the fastest lever a retail lender has. Underwriting changes take 12-18 months to show in the P&L; a collections capacity change shows up in next month's roll rates. It is also the function where credit risk becomes a conduct risk. RBI's fair practices and outsourcing directions constrain recovery agent behaviour - permissible contact hours, prohibition on harassment or contacting persons outside the specified reference list, mandatory identification of agents, and grievance redress. A collections process that lifts recovery while generating complaints is not a success; it is a supervisory exposure and, for digital lenders, a licensing risk.

## Formula

Bucket-wise strategy follows the economics of contact cost against balance at risk:

| Bucket | Typical treatment | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 0 DPD, pre-due | SMS and IVR reminders, auto-debit mandate check | Prevent technical delinquency |
| 1-30 DPD | Tele-calling, digital nudges, payment links | Cure before the account becomes a habit |
| 31-60 DPD | Intensive tele-calling, allocation to field for high balances | Prevent hardening |
| 61-90 DPD | Field visits, restructuring assessment, settlement authority | Avoid NPA classification |
| 90+ DPD | Agency placement, legal notice, repossession for secured | Maximise recovery cash |

Treatment decisions are made on expected value, not on collectability alone:

```
Net benefit = (Delta_roll x Pool balance x Downstream loss factor x LGD) - (n x Cost per account)
```

where:
- `Delta_roll` = reduction in the forward roll rate attributable to the treatment, in percentage points
- `Pool balance` = total outstanding in the bucket being treated, in ₹
- `Downstream loss factor` = product of the remaining forward roll rates from the next bucket to default, in %
- `LGD` = loss given default, in %
- `n` = number of accounts treated
- `Cost per account` = fully loaded treatment cost including repeat attempts, in ₹

Settlement decisions are a present-value comparison:

```
Accept settlement if: (Offer x (1 - Agency fee)) > PV of expected recovery from continued pursuit
```

Write-off is an accounting action, not an economic one. A **technical write-off** removes the asset from the balance sheet while the lender retains the legal right to recover; recovery effort and bureau reporting continue. It should never be read as a decision to stop collecting.

## Worked Example

Example: allocating a fixed collections budget across two buckets on the personal loan book, using the roll rates from 1.9 and an LGD of 72%.

Option A - intensify Bucket 1 (1-30 DPD). Pool: 4,200 accounts, average balance ₹2,18,000, total ₹91.56 crore. Treatment lowers the forward roll from 31.0% to 27.9%, a 3.1 pp reduction. Downstream loss factor from Bucket 2 onwards = 58.5% x 76.0% = 44.46%. Cost ₹340 per account for a multi-attempt calling cycle.

```
Balance prevented from rolling = 3.1% x ₹91.56 cr = ₹2.84 cr
Loss avoided = ₹2.84 cr x 44.46% x 72% = ₹90.9 lakh
Cost = 4,200 x ₹340 = ₹14.28 lakh
Net benefit = ₹76.6 lakh    Benefit-to-cost ratio = 6.4x
```

Option B - intensify Bucket 3 (61-90 DPD). Pool: 380 accounts, average balance ₹2,64,000, total ₹10.03 crore. Field visits plus settlement authority lower the forward roll from 76.0% to 68.4%, a 7.6 pp reduction. These accounts roll straight to default, so the downstream factor is 100%. Cost ₹3,950 per account, covering an average 2.5 field attempts plus legal notice.

```
Balance prevented from rolling = 7.6% x ₹10.03 cr = ₹76.2 lakh
Loss avoided = ₹76.2 lakh x 100% x 72% = ₹54.9 lakh
Cost = 380 x ₹3,950 = ₹15.01 lakh
Net benefit = ₹39.9 lakh    Benefit-to-cost ratio = 3.7x
```

Bucket 1 wins on both absolute benefit and efficiency, because the pool is nine times larger and the treatment is twelve times cheaper per account. The deeper bucket has a higher per-account impact but too few accounts and too high a unit cost to compete. This is the general shape of the result in unsecured retail, and it is why mature collections operations concentrate capacity in the first thirty days.

Settlement evaluation. Take the defaulted account from 1.4 with an EAD of ₹3,34,110. The borrower offers a one-time settlement of 42% of outstanding.

```
Settlement offer      = ₹3,34,110 x 42%   = ₹1,40,326
Less agency fee at 12%                    = (₹16,839)
Net cash today                            = ₹1,23,487

PV of continued pursuit (from 1.3, 16.46% net discounted RR)
                      = ₹3,34,110 x 16.46% = ₹54,995
```

Settlement dominates by ₹68,492. Two things must still be checked before accepting: the account is reported to bureaus as settled rather than closed, which carries a real and lasting consequence for the borrower and should be disclosed clearly; and settlement rates must be monitored for gaming, since a generous settlement policy that becomes known will pull otherwise-payable accounts into delinquency deliberately.

## How it connects

Collections performance is measured in the roll rates of 1.9 and realised in the LGD of 1.3 - a collections improvement is an LGD improvement, and flows through expected loss (1.5) and pricing (1.7). Restructuring decisions interact directly with IFRS9 staging (1.11), since a modification is a strong SICR indicator, and with RBI IRAC asset classification and provisioning (1.15). For secured products, the enforcement toolkit - repossession, SARFAESI action on mortgages - is the subject of 1.13.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Measuring collections by rupees collected. Gross collections rise automatically with portfolio size and delinquency. The correct metrics are bucket-wise roll rate, cure rate, and re-default rate on cured accounts.
- Paying agencies a flat percentage of gross collections. This incentivises cherry-picking easy accounts and abandoning hard ones. Tiered commissions by bucket and vintage, with resolution-rate floors, align the agency to the lender's actual loss curve.
- Using restructuring as a delinquency-management tool. Repeatedly restructuring the same borrower moves the account out of the delinquency buckets without improving the borrower's capacity. It suppresses every downstream metric and is the mechanism behind evergreening. Restructured exposure must be tracked as a separate pool with its own re-default reporting.
- Reading technical write-off as loss realisation. The recovery right survives, post-write-off recoveries are a real income line, and treating write-off as an endpoint understates ultimate recovery rates.
- Ignoring re-default on cured accounts. An account cured in Bucket 1 that re-enters delinquency within three months was not cured; it was deferred. Re-default rates above roughly 25% indicate the treatment is extracting one payment rather than resolving the underlying position.
