# 1.15 NPA-NPL Management (India-specific)

## Definition

Under RBI's Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning (IRAC) norms, a loan becomes a **non-performing asset (NPA)** when interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days. Before that point, accounts are flagged as **special mention accounts (SMA)** - SMA-0 at 1-30 days overdue, SMA-1 at 31-60, SMA-2 at 61-90. Once classified NPA, the asset moves through sub-categories over time: sub-standard for the first 12 months, doubtful thereafter (D1, D2, D3 by age), and loss assets where recovery is negligible. Provisioning follows classification mechanically, by prescribed percentage rather than by estimated loss.

## Why it matters

IRAC is an incurred-loss framework with rule-based provisioning, which makes it predictable but blunt. It also produces the headline numbers by which Indian lenders are judged - GNPA, NNPA, provision coverage ratio, slippage ratio, credit cost - and those numbers move share prices and supervisory attention. Two clarifications from RBI's November 2021 circular sharply tightened the regime: DPD must be stamped daily as part of day-end processing rather than at month-end, and an NPA can be upgraded to standard only when the entire arrears of interest and principal are cleared, not merely on part-payment bringing the account below 90 days. Both closed practices that had allowed accounts to be managed around reporting dates. This regime is now finite: RBI's Expected Credit Loss Directions, finalised in April 2026, replace IRAC provisioning for scheduled commercial banks from 1 April 2027, with a glide path to 31 March 2031.

## Formula

Classification and minimum provisioning for retail advances:

| Classification | Trigger | Minimum provision |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Not overdue beyond 90 days | 0.40% general (lower for agri/MSME) |
| Sub-standard, secured | NPA up to 12 months | 15% of outstanding |
| Sub-standard, unsecured | NPA up to 12 months | 25% of outstanding |
| Doubtful D1, secured portion | Doubtful up to 1 year | 25% of secured portion |
| Doubtful D2, secured portion | Doubtful 1-3 years | 40% of secured portion |
| Doubtful D3, secured portion | Doubtful over 3 years | 100% of secured portion |
| Doubtful, unsecured portion | Any | 100% |
| Loss asset | Identified as unrealisable | 100% |

Personal loans, credit cards, and BNPL sit almost entirely in the unsecured columns, so a retail unsecured NPA reaches 25% provisioning immediately and 100% within 24 months of the default date.

Portfolio metrics:

```
GNPA %          = Gross NPA / Gross advances
NNPA %          = (Gross NPA - provisions held on NPAs) / (Gross advances - provisions held on NPAs)
PCR             = Provisions held on NPAs / Gross NPA
Slippage ratio  = Fresh slippages during the period / Opening standard advances
Credit cost     = Provisions charged to P&L / Average gross advances

Closing GNPA = Opening GNPA + Slippages - Upgrades - Recoveries - Write-offs
```

Income recognition stops at NPA classification. Interest accrued but not realised on an account that becomes NPA must be reversed from income if it was booked in the current or prior periods.

## Worked Example

Example A - account-level provisioning under IRAC versus ECL. The defaulted personal loan from 1.4, outstanding ₹3,34,110, unsecured.

| Time from default | IRAC classification | IRAC provision | Ind AS 109 ECL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 89 | Standard (SMA-2) | ₹1,336 (0.40%) | ₹7,804 (lifetime, Stage 2) |
| Day 91 | Sub-standard, unsecured | ₹83,528 (25%) | Lifetime ECL, Stage 3 |
| Month 13 | Doubtful, unsecured | ₹3,34,110 (100%) | Lifetime ECL, Stage 3 |

The contrast is the whole argument for the transition. IRAC provisions ₹1,336 on day 89 and ₹83,528 on day 91 - a 62-fold jump triggered by a calendar event, having recognised almost nothing during the three months of visible deterioration that preceded it. ECL recognises loss progressively as credit risk increases and produces no cliff. IRAC then overshoots in the other direction, holding 100% against an account whose realistic discounted recovery, from 1.3, is around 16.5% of exposure.

Example B - portfolio metrics for a retail book over a financial year.

```
Opening gross advances       = ₹1,704.2 cr
Opening gross NPA            =    ₹49.2 cr
Opening standard advances    = ₹1,655.0 cr

Fresh slippages              =    ₹62.4 cr
Upgrades                     =     ₹8.1 cr
Cash recoveries              =    ₹11.3 cr
Write-offs (including technical) = ₹38.6 cr

Closing gross NPA = 49.2 + 62.4 - 8.1 - 11.3 - 38.6 = ₹53.6 cr
Closing gross advances = ₹1,840.0 cr
Provisions held on NPAs = ₹34.9 cr
```

Derived metrics:

```
GNPA %         = 53.6 / 1,840.0                      = 2.91%
PCR            = 34.9 / 53.6                         = 65.11%
NNPA %         = (53.6 - 34.9) / (1,840.0 - 34.9)    = 1.04%
Slippage ratio = 62.4 / 1,655.0                      = 3.77%
```

The GNPA of 2.91% looks contained. The slippage ratio of 3.77% is the number that matters - it says fresh NPA formation over the year exceeded the closing stock, and that the stock stayed low only because ₹38.6 crore was written off. Strip out the write-offs and GNPA would be 5.01%. This is the standard reason to read slippage and write-off alongside GNPA rather than GNPA alone.

## Common Pitfalls / Misconceptions

- Reading GNPA as a measure of asset quality. It is a stock net of write-offs and can be reduced to any desired level by writing off faster. Slippage ratio and credit cost are the flow measures that cannot be managed this way.
- Treating technical write-off as a closed account. The recovery right survives and post-write-off recoveries are real income, but the exposure has left every asset-quality metric. Comparing PCR across lenders without adjusting for differing technical write-off policy is meaningless.
- Assuming part-payment upgrades an NPA. Since the November 2021 clarification, upgrade requires clearance of the entire outstanding arrears. Bringing an account from 110 DPD to 85 DPD does not restore standard classification.
- Applying facility-level classification. Where a borrower has multiple facilities with the same lender, classification is applied at the borrower level - one NPA facility taints the others.
- Confusing restructuring with cure. Under the June 2019 Prudential Framework on Resolution of Stressed Assets, restructuring a retail account generally results in downgrade, with upgrade permitted only after satisfactory performance across a specified period. Restructuring to avoid classification is evergreening and is treated as such by supervisors.
- Forgetting the income reversal. Interest booked on an account that subsequently becomes NPA must be reversed, so a slippage hits both the provision line and the interest income line in the same quarter.
